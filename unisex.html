Our Approach

Scent Has No Gender

Category labels are a shorthand, not a rule. Oud, amber, leather and musk are the same molecules on everyone — what changes is the wearer, the climate and the moment. The fragrances in this edit were composed without a specific direction in mind.

Some lean slightly warm, some slightly fresh. None of them are decorated to look masculine or feminine. They are simply what they are, and they work on anyone who likes the composition.

  • Oud & Amber Deep, resinous compositions for evening wear.
  • Fresh & Clean Citrus and green notes for warmer weather.
  • Soft & Skin Scents Musk and iris compositions that sit close.

Wearing Guide

How To Wear

Every fragrance is composed of the same three layers — top, heart and base — regardless of who is intended to wear it. Historically, "masculine" scents leaned woody and "feminine" scents leaned floral, but that split is a marketing convention, not a chemical one. If a composition appeals to you, it belongs to you.
Oud and amber are the two heaviest families in most catalogs. If you are new to them, start with a composition where they sit in the base rather than the heart — you get the warmth without the weight. Royal Oud and Amber Dusk are good starting points.
A soft musk like Musk Serenity is one of the best layering fragrances in any collection. Apply it first as a base, then spray any other composition over it. The musk extends the wear of whatever is on top and makes the transition between day and evening smoother.
Unisex compositions often smell different on two people because the same fragrance reacts to different skin chemistry. A fragrance that reads warm on one person may read fresher on another. This is not a defect — it is one of the more interesting things about how perfume works.
