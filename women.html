Our Approach

Softness With Structure

Floral fragrance is often dismissed as something simple. We see it differently — the most refined feminine scents are built the same way a good song is: a clear opening, a memorable middle, and a quiet end that stays with you.

Rose is allowed to breathe. Jasmine is anchored by musk. Peony and iris bridge the bright top notes to the amber and vanilla base. Nothing competes. Everything composes.

  • Composed Florals Rose, jasmine, peony and iris in balance.
  • Bright Openings Pear, citrus and lychee that settle cleanly.
  • Warm Bases Amber, vanilla and musk for a lingering finish.

Wearing Guide

How To Wear

Apply to pulse points — the wrists, the base of the throat and behind the ears. Floral fragrances especially benefit from warm skin, so allow the composition to settle for a minute before you judge the opening. Avoid rubbing.
Light florals layer beautifully with a soft musk or a single spritz of amber. Apply the deeper fragrance first (base layer) and the floral on top (opening layer). This is a common technique for making a light composition last longer across the day.
Bright, fruity florals read best in spring and summer. Ambered florals with rose, vanilla and spice come into their own in cooler weather. If you only own one, a rose and musk composition works year-round in most climates.
Light florals fade faster than woody or amber compositions — this is normal. Apply unscented moisturiser first, spray on the wrists and the base of the throat, and reapply once in the late afternoon if needed. A matching body lotion extends wear significantly.
